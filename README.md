# High Thermal Mass Electric Heater – Concept Design

This repository contains an early-stage **conceptual design** for a high thermal mass electric space heater.

The purpose of this project is to explore alternative electric heater architectures that prioritize:
- thermal inertia,
- radiant heat contribution,
- and calm, sustained heat delivery,

in contrast to conventional low-mass, high-convection electric heaters.

This work is intentionally published at an **early conceptual stage** in order to establish clear public prior art and enable open, non-exclusive development.

---

## Design Intent

Conventional electric heaters typically exhibit:
- rapid temperature cycling,
- harsh convective airflow,
- limited thermal storage,
- and weak radiant heat characteristics.

This concept investigates whether an electric heater can approach the **thermal behavior and user experience of traditional Anatolian wood or coal stoves**, while remaining electrically powered.

Key goals:
- Increased thermal mass near the heat source
- Delayed and sustained heat release
- Reduced reliance on forced airflow
- Active heat radiation from external metal surfaces

---

## Concept Overview

The initial design is based on a **vertical cylindrical architecture** and includes:

- A refractory firebrick core acting as the primary thermal mass
- Resistance heating elements guided by external grooves
- A hollow internal channel supporting natural upward airflow
- An upper refractory element exposed to rising hot air
- A cast iron or thick steel top plate acting as a radiant heat spreader
- An external metal enclosure intended to actively absorb and radiate heat

The design combines:
- Radiant heating from hot surfaces
- Natural convection without forced airflow
- Thermal buffering through refractory materials

---

## Firebrick Core Variants

Two firebrick core variants are intentionally documented and published together:

### 1. Pocketed (High Thermal Mass) Variant

- Cylindrical firebrick with internal material pockets
- Intended to explore increased thermal inertia
- Higher conceptual complexity and manufacturing risk
- Included to explicitly document this design direction as prior art

### 2. Plain Hollow Cylindrical Variant

- Uniform cylindrical firebrick without internal pockets
- Simpler geometry
- Lower manufacturing risk
- More suitable for modular construction and open hardware replication

Both variants represent the **same underlying design concept**, differing only in thermal mass distribution strategy.

---

## External Enclosure and Heat Radiation

A core objective of this design is that **the external metal enclosure is an active heat-radiating element**, not merely a protective shell.

Accordingly:
- Full thermal isolation between the refractory core and enclosure is avoided
- Rigid mechanical coupling that would cause thermal stress is also avoided

The enclosure is intended to function as a secondary radiant surface.

---

## Safety Considerations

This repository **does not provide a certified, production-ready, or consumer-safe heater design**.

Any practical implementation must include, at minimum:
- Tip-over electrical cut-off
- Independent over-temperature protection
- Appropriate thermostatic control
- Compliance with all applicable electrical and fire safety regulations

Any construction or experimentation based on this material is performed **entirely at the user’s own risk**.

---

## Power Level Considerations

The design explores operation in the ~2000 W class.

Perceived heating effectiveness is expected to depend strongly on:
- geometry,
- surface area,
- and radiant contribution,

rather than electrical input power alone.

No final electrical specification is defined at this stage.
